## Top recruiter jobs

1. **Get me an interview-ready shortlist fast.**  
Early-career recruiting breaks when application volume scales across campuses and recruiters spend more time filtering and coordinating than evaluating talent. Inference: The highest-value job is not “store applications,” but “reduce a noisy funnel to 20 candidates I trust enough to interview.” [builtinsf](https://www.builtinsf.com/articles/handshake-raises-200m-3b-valuation-hiring)

2. **Standardize messy campus data into one comparable view.**  
Superset’s 2026 campus hiring guide says candidate data comes in inconsistent formats across universities and shortlisting slows under time pressure. Inference: Recruiters need normalization before they need more automation. [builtinsf](https://www.builtinsf.com/articles/handshake-raises-200m-3b-valuation-hiring)

3. **Move qualified candidates into the hiring process without coordination chaos.**  
Interview coordination is described as the biggest bottleneck once hiring scales, but it happens after the shortlist problem. Inference: Scheduling matters, but it is not the best opening wedge because it solves a downstream symptom. [builtinsf](https://www.builtinsf.com/articles/handshake-raises-200m-3b-valuation-hiring)

## Best wedge product

The best wedge is **Explainable Shortlist Generator for early-career engineering hiring**. It should ingest student profiles, GitHub, projects, certifications, college/branch eligibility, and optional assessment signals, then output a ranked shortlist with recruiter-facing reasons like “strong backend project depth,” “consistent GitHub activity,” “Java + DSA fit,” or “meets campus eligibility and interview readiness threshold”. [g2](https://www.g2.com/products/ripplematch/reviews/ripplematch-review-4916228)

Fact: Recruiters already use Handshake, Yello, Symplicity, ATS platforms, and spreadsheets for campus workflows. Inference: KodeTalent should not replace those first; it should sit in front of them and answer one painful question better than anyone else: **who should I interview right now?** [gitnux](https://gitnux.org/best/campus-recruiting-software/)

### Must-have

- One requisition intake: role, skills, graduation year, locations, compensation band, must-have filters.
- Candidate normalization from KodeTalent profiles into a consistent schema.
- Ranked shortlist of top 20–100 candidates.
- Transparent scoring reasons, not black-box AI.
- One-click export to CSV / ATS / email outreach list.
- Optional recruiter feedback loop: thumbs up/down to improve ranking.

### Not build yet

- Full ATS.
- Full interview scheduling suite.
- Multi-campus event management.
- Heavy outreach automation.
- Full college procurement workflow.
- Broad resume verification as a separate product.
- Generic AI matching with no explainability.

## One-meeting sale

The easiest one-meeting sale is: **“Give us one role. We will return an interview-ready shortlist from verified engineering talent in 48 hours.”** This is easier to buy than a platform transformation because it is narrow, measurable, and maps directly to recruiter pain around slow shortlisting and fragmented data. [builtinsf](https://www.builtinsf.com/articles/handshake-raises-200m-3b-valuation-hiring)

Fact: Handshake’s employer product is positioned around getting jobs in front of millions of students, but review text also says the posting experience can be confusing and noisy. Inference: A faster-closing new entrant should avoid selling “another campus platform” and instead sell a **decision product**—shortlist quality, not system complexity. [joinsuperset](https://joinsuperset.com/blogs/from-degree-completion-to-job-confirmation-how-online-placement-really-functions-inside-universities/)

## Best pilot offer

The fastest pilot offer is:

- One role, one geography, one graduating cohort.
- KodeTalent delivers top 25 or top 50 candidates.
- Candidates include verified evidence and recruiter-ready notes.
- Recruiter gets shortlist export plus optional warm outreach support.
- Promise a fixed turnaround, e.g. 48–72 hours.

This works because it sells an outcome, not software shelfware. Inference: Recruiters approve budget faster for “fill this funnel now” than for “adopt a new multi-sided platform.”

## Pricing suggestion

The most realistic early pricing model is **success-based pilot, then subscription or usage hybrid**. Fact: Employer plans and premium early-talent products already monetize recruiters rather than students on platforms like Handshake and RippleMatch. [ripplematch](https://ripplematch.com/employers)

Recommended structure, as inference:
- Pilot: flat fee per role or per shortlist batch.
- Expansion: monthly seat fee for recruiter access plus usage-based charge per active role or contacted shortlisted candidate.
- Avoid pure per-hire only at the start; it delays cash and makes you look like an agency.
- Avoid enterprise annual contracts too early; that hurts one-meeting conversion.
